Sources

Every number on this site traces back to one of the items below. Where the evidence is strong the page states things plainly. Where it is thin or contested, the page says so instead of pretending otherwise.

How to read this list

Meta-analyses and systematic reviews pool many studies and sit near the top of the evidence hierarchy for training and nutrition questions. Position stands are expert consensus over that evidence. Public health guidelines set population floors rather than optimal targets. Single trials, however well run, are the weakest item here and are marked as such.

What we are confident about, and what we are not

Settled

Calorie balance drives weight change. Protein around 1.6 g/kg supports muscle. More weekly sets produce more growth up to a point. Training closer to failure produces more growth. Creatine works.

Reasonably clear

Twice weekly frequency slightly beats once. Longer rest beats short rest. Load matters little for hypertrophy if sets are hard. The exact upper limit of useful volume varies a lot between people.

Genuinely uncertain

Optimal volume for a given individual. How much lengthened-position training matters. Long-run effects of very high protein. Most supplement claims beyond creatine and caffeine.

Two honest caveats

Most of this research is short. Typical training studies run 8 to 12 weeks in untrained or moderately trained people. Extrapolating to a decade of training in a well-trained lifter involves judgement, not just data.

Group averages are not you. Every number here is a population midpoint with real variation around it. The four week review loop on the start page exists precisely because your own data beats any published average.

The list

Links go to PubMed or the publishing body. Citation numbers match the superscript markers used throughout the site.

  1. Mifflin MD, St Jeor ST, Hill LA, Scott BJ, Daugherty SA, Koh YO (1990). A new predictive equation for resting energy expenditure in healthy individuals. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. Validation study

    The equation behind the calorie calculator. Still the most accurate of the common prediction equations for the general population, and still routinely off by around 10 to 15% for a given individual. PubMed

  2. Hall KD, Heymsfield SB, Kemnitz JW, Klein S, Schoeller DA, Speakman JR (2012). Energy balance and its components: implications for body weight regulation. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. Review

    Why energy balance determines body weight, and why the relationship is dynamic rather than a fixed arithmetic subtraction. PubMed

  3. Morton RW, Murphy KT, McKellar SR, et al. (2018). A systematic review, meta-analysis and meta-regression of the effect of protein supplementation on resistance training-induced gains in muscle mass and strength in healthy adults. British Journal of Sports Medicine. Meta-analysis

    The source of the protein figure. Benefits for muscle mass plateaued at roughly 1.6 g per kg per day, with a confidence interval extending to about 2.2, which is where the range on this site comes from. PubMed

  4. Jäger R, Kerksick CM, Campbell BI, et al. (2017). International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand: protein and exercise. Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition. Position stand

    Protein distribution across meals, protein quality, and plant versus animal sources. PubMed

  5. Helms ER, Aragon AA, Fitschen PJ (2014). Evidence-based recommendations for natural bodybuilding contest preparation: nutrition and supplementation. Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition. Review

    The original tiered model this site borrows its structure from, plus the case for higher protein while in a deficit. PubMed

  6. Gardner CD, Trepanowski JF, Del Gobbo LC, et al. (2018). Effect of low-fat vs low-carbohydrate diet on 12-month weight loss in overweight adults (DIETFITS). JAMA. Randomised trial

    609 adults, 12 months, no significant difference in weight loss between low-fat and low-carbohydrate diets, and no useful predictive effect from genotype or insulin secretion. A single trial, but a large and well-run one. PubMed

  7. U.S. Departments of Agriculture and Health and Human Services (2020). Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2020 to 2025. Federal guideline. Guideline

    Source of the fibre target, roughly 14 g per 1,000 kcal, and the general whole-food pattern advice. dietaryguidelines.gov

  8. Kreider RB, Kalman DS, Antonio J, et al. (2017). International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand: safety and efficacy of creatine supplementation in exercise, sport, and medicine. Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition. Position stand

    Dose, form, the absence of any need for a loading phase, and the safety record at 3 to 5 g per day in healthy adults. PubMed

  9. Guest NS, VanDusseldorp TA, Nelson MT, et al. (2021). International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand: caffeine and exercise performance. Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition. Position stand

    The 3 to 6 mg per kg dose and the 30 to 60 minute timing window. PubMed

  10. Aragon AA, Schoenfeld BJ (2013). Nutrient timing revisited: is there a post-exercise anabolic window? Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition. Review

    Why the anabolic window is hours wide rather than minutes, and why total daily protein matters far more than its timing. PubMed

  11. Schoenfeld BJ, Ogborn D, Krieger JW (2017). Dose-response relationship between weekly resistance training volume and increases in muscle mass: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Sports Sciences. Meta-analysis

    The basis for the 10 to 20 weekly sets guidance. More sets produced more growth across the studied range. The upper limit before returns turn negative is not well established and almost certainly varies between individuals. PubMed

  12. Schoenfeld BJ, Ogborn D, Krieger JW (2016). Effects of resistance training frequency on measures of muscle hypertrophy: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Sports Medicine. Meta-analysis

    Training a muscle twice a week outperformed once a week. The effect is real but modest, and largely disappears once weekly volume is equated, which is why this site treats frequency as a scheduling convenience. PubMed

  13. Schoenfeld BJ, Grgic J, Ogborn D, Krieger JW (2017). Strength and hypertrophy adaptations between low- versus high-load resistance training: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research. Meta-analysis

    Similar hypertrophy across a wide load range when sets are taken near failure. Maximal strength still favours heavier loads. PubMed

  14. Refalo MC, Helms ER, Trexler ET, Hamilton DL, Fyfe JJ (2023). Influence of resistance training proximity-to-failure on skeletal muscle hypertrophy: a systematic review with meta-analysis. Sports Medicine. Meta-analysis

    Hypertrophy improved as sets were taken closer to failure, with the benefit flattening in the final couple of reps. The basis for the 1 to 3 reps in reserve guidance rather than training to failure on everything. PubMed

  15. Schoenfeld BJ, Pope ZK, Benik FM, et al. (2016). Longer interset rest periods enhance muscle strength and hypertrophy in resistance-trained men. Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research. Randomised trial

    Three minute rest beat one minute for both strength and size, overturning the older short-rest advice. The mechanism is simply more reps completed at a given load. PubMed

  16. Helms ER, Cronin J, Storey A, Zourdos MC (2016). Application of the repetitions in reserve-based rating of perceived exertion scale for resistance training. Strength and Conditioning Journal. Applied review

    Where the reps in reserve scale used throughout this site comes from, and its limits. Novices systematically underestimate how close to failure they actually are. PubMed

  17. World Health Organization (2020). WHO guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour. Global guideline. Guideline

    The 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity per week floor, plus muscle strengthening on two or more days a week. who.int

  18. Paluch AE, Bajpai S, Bassett DR, et al. (2022). Daily steps and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis of 15 international cohorts. The Lancet Public Health. Meta-analysis

    Mortality risk fell with more daily steps, levelling off around 6,000 to 8,000 for older adults and 8,000 to 10,000 for younger adults. This is observational data, so it shows association rather than proof of cause. PubMed

  19. Kodama S, Saito K, Tanaka S, et al. (2009). Cardiorespiratory fitness as a quantitative predictor of all-cause mortality and cardiovascular events in healthy men and women: a meta-analysis. JAMA. Meta-analysis

    Why cardio is not optional even if you train for appearance. Observational, so the same causal caveat applies. PubMed

  20. Nedeltcheva AV, Kilkus JM, Imperial J, Schoeller DA, Penev PD (2010). Insufficient sleep undermines dietary efforts to reduce adiposity. Annals of Internal Medicine. Randomised crossover trial

    Same calorie deficit, less sleep, and a much larger share of the lost weight came from lean mass rather than fat. A small study, but the reason sleep sits in the foundation tier on this site. PubMed

Corrections

If something here is wrong or out of date, it should be fixed rather than defended. The evidence base moves, particularly on training volume and proximity to failure, where good work has been published recently and more is coming.

Pages carry a last reviewed date in the sidebar. Anything not reviewed in the last year should be treated with more caution than the confident tone suggests.
