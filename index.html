Fitness fundamentals, ranked by what actually matters

Most fitness advice is not wrong. It is unranked. A supplement stack and your weekly calorie intake get the same airtime, so everything feels equally urgent and nothing gets done. This site sorts nutrition and training into three tiers so you can tell the 5% that drives the result from the 95% that does not.

Get my numbers Pick a training plan
Short answer

If you do only seven things, do these: eat a deliberate calorie target, get 1.6 to 2.2 g of protein per kg of bodyweight, lift 2 to 4 times a week covering all six movement patterns, take your sets to within 1 to 3 reps of failure, write down every set so you can beat it next time, sleep 7 to 9 hours, and walk 7,000 or more steps a day.

Everything else on this site is detail. Everything the industry sells you is a variation on one of those seven lines.

How the tiers work

Each topic is scored by how much it changes your result. The colour tells you when to care about it.

Green: foundation

Four or five things. Get them roughly right, hold them for months, and you will outpace almost everyone who is busy optimising the tiers above.

Worth about 80% of the result.

Amber: worth tuning

Real and measurable, but only once the foundation runs without willpower. Tuning these first is how people stall for years while doing everything right.

Worth about 15 to 20%.

Red: ignore for now

Timing, tempo, supplements, advanced techniques. A percent or two for an advanced lifter and nothing at all for anyone else. This is where the noise lives.

Worth about 1 to 5%.

The two pyramids

Nutrition

Calorie balance and protein carry almost all of it. Macro splits, fibre and food quality come next. Supplements and meal timing come last, and mostly do not matter.

Training

Showing up, training hard and adding weight over time beat every programming detail. Volume, frequency and exercise choice come next. Rest periods and tempo come last.

Set up in an hour

  1. Decide how many days you can protect

    Not aspire to. Protect, on a bad week. For most people with a full-time job that is three. Pick the matching plan and put the sessions in your calendar as recurring events with a location attached.

  2. Get your calorie and protein numbers

    Two minutes in the calculator. Write the two numbers somewhere you will see them daily. Ignore every other part of your diet for now.

  3. Set up a training log

    A notes app works. One line per set: exercise, weight, reps. This is the highest return habit on the site and the one most people skip. There is a free printable log if you prefer paper.

  4. Read the cues for your main lifts only

    Four or five movements from the library, not the whole page. You will pick up the rest as you go.

  5. Weigh yourself tomorrow morning, then daily

    Same conditions each time, after the toilet and before food. You care about the weekly average, never the daily number.

The first four weeks

Four things. Do not add, optimise or research anything during this period. The goal is to prove to yourself that you can hold a simple thing steady for a month.

1. Hit your protein every day

The number from the calculator. Front load it at breakfast if you tend to fall short by evening. This alone shifts body composition more than anything else you could start with.

2. Complete every session

All of them. A short session beats a skipped one. If you have 25 minutes, do the first two exercises and leave. Never skip because you cannot do the full thing.

3. Take your last set close to failure

One to three reps left. If you are not sure what that feels like, take one isolation exercise all the way to failure once this week so you have a reference point.

4. Walk 7,000 or more steps

Or 2,000 more than your current normal. The cheapest lever on daily energy expenditure, and it does not interfere with recovery.18

Deliberately not on that list

Supplements, meal timing, splits, cardio protocols, macro ratios, weighing every gram, and anything you saw on Instagram. All of it is amber or red. Adding it now costs you attention you need for the four things above, and attention is the real constraint.

Week five: the first review

Compare your week one average weight to your week four average. Then look at your log and ask whether the numbers went up on most lifts.

Weight flat when you wanted lossYour real maintenance is lower than the estimate, or the food logging is optimisticCut 150 to 200 kcal, or add 2,000 steps. One or the other, not both.

Only after that month should you read nutrition tier two and training tier two. You will get more out of them having done the basics first.

Where a coach still earns their fee

Everything here is the general case, true for most people most of the time. That covers more ground than the industry likes to admit, but it is not everything. Go and find a qualified human when:

  • You are rehabbing an injury, managing pain, or working around a medical condition or medication.
  • You are pregnant or postpartum, or your situation has a clinical dimension a website cannot see.
  • You compete, or you are chasing a specific number on a specific date.
  • You have applied the green tier honestly for six months and genuinely stalled.
  • You know what to do and still do not do it. That is the most common one, and it is a coaching problem rather than an information problem.

Why this site exists

No products, no affiliate links, no email capture, no tracking scripts. Every recommendation points at a meta-analysis, position stand or public health guideline, and where the evidence is weak the page says so. If something here is wrong, the sources page is the fastest way to check it.
